WHY
The idea, in one breath

Launching an AI agent straight onto the open internet is like throwing a baby crocodile into the open ocean. It is not ready. The crocodile solved this problem before dinosaurs went extinct: raise the young in five careful stages. CULTUS simply copied the homework.

SCALE
First — meet the teacher

The largest reptile
on Earth.

Seven metres long. A bite stronger than a lion's, a tiger's, and a shark's — combined. And yet, with its babies, it is one of the most careful parents in the animal kingdom. That contrast — terrifying power, infinite tenderness — is the entire design philosophy of the harness you are about to read.

In plain words Strong enough to crush anything. Gentle enough to carry a baby in its teeth. Good infrastructure should feel exactly like that.

NEST
Stage 1 of 5 · The Nest

Where you grow up
decides what you become.

A crocodile nest is a compost heap that warms itself. The temperature of that mound decides whether each egg hatches male or female — the nest literally designs its babies. SALTWATER does the same with agents: their environment is chosen first, and it shapes everything they become.

In plain words An agent's settings aren't a form it fills in later. They're the womb it grows in. Get the environment right before the agent ever wakes up.

SW-01
# nest/config.py — the womb comes first
from saltwater import Nest

nest = Nest(
    medium="estuarine",        # where it grows up
    temperature=31.6,          # decides its traits
    guardrails=True,
)

with nest.incubate() as env:
    agent = env.clutch("harvester-07")  # born already shaped
Acquisition nest/config.py · environment as womb
CALL
Stage 2 of 5 · The Vocalize

They agree to be born
together.

Hours before hatching, the babies start calling from inside their eggs — and the calls spread, egg to egg, until the whole clutch hatches in the same hour. Nobody comes out alone into a dangerous world. SALTWATER agents do this too: before a group of agents launches, they check each other's readiness and emerge all at once, or not at all.

In plain words It's a headcount before the big moment. If your launch depends on ten agents and only seven are ready, the crocodile says: wait.

SW-02
# vocalize/consensus.py — hatch together or not at all
from saltwater import Vocalize

round = Vocalize(clutch=agents, quorum=0.75)

async with round.listen() as chorus:
    await chorus.until_ready(timeout="30s")
    await chorus.hatch()   # all, or none
Acquisition vocalize/consensus.py · nobody emerges alone
JAWS
Stage 3 of 5 · The Transport

The deadliest mouth on Earth
becomes a cradle.

The mother scoops her hatchlings into her jaws — the same jaws that crush buffalo — and carries them through mud, roots and predators to safe water, without leaving a scratch. SALTWATER moves young agents the same way: the most powerful, highest-privilege part of the system escorts the fragile new agents across the dangerous parts of the network — then lets go.

In plain words Power isn't for showing off; it's for carrying fragile things safely. Your strongest systems should escort your weakest ones.

SW-03
# transport/maternal.py — jaws as vehicle
from saltwater import Transport

escort = Transport.jaws(
    route=["nest", "bank", "shallows"],
    bite_pressure=0.0,   # maximal strength, zero harm
)

await escort.carry(agents).release_at("estuary")
Acquisition transport/maternal.py · power in service of fragility
NURSERY
Stage 4 of 5 · The Estuary

Practice in water that's real —
but fenced.

The young crocodiles grow up in an estuary: real fish, real currents, real stakes — but no sharks, because the open ocean can't get in. A training ground that's honest but survivable. SALTWATER's staging works the same: agents handle real traffic inside fences that block anything irreversible — and the fences fade, one by one, as the agent proves itself.

In plain words A sandbox that lies teaches nothing. Let agents practice on the real thing, behind guardrails that quietly disappear as they earn it.

SW-04
# estuary/nursery.py — a true ocean, fenced
from saltwater import Estuary

nursery = Estuary(
    live_traffic=True,              # real stakes
    fence=["irreversible_writes"],   # honest, not fatal
)

nursery.fade(policy="demonstrated_competence")
Acquisition estuary/nursery.py · safety that knows how to leave
RELEASE
Stage 5 of 5 · Open Water

Then she swims away —
and that is the care.

When the young are ready, the mother leaves. Not because she stops caring — because a crocodile that never hunts alone never becomes a crocodile. The whole five-stage journey exists to make the leaving safe. SALTWATER releases agents the same way: full freedom, granted piece by piece, exactly as fast as competence is proven — and not one heartbeat faster.

In plain words An agent that needs its guardrails forever wasn't raised — it was stored. The goal of every safety system is the day it's no longer needed.

SW-05
# open_water/release.py — graduated abandonment
from saltwater import OpenWater

sea = OpenWater(agents)

async for j in sea.graduate(metric="competence"):
    if j.thrives():
        sea.withdraw(j)   # it hunts alone now.
                          # that was the point.
Acquisition open_water/release.py · the reward for good guardrails is their absence
FIVE
The whole journey, memorized in one breath

Nest — grow them in the right environment.
Vocalize — emerge together, or not at all.
Transport — let power carry fragility.
Estuary — practice for real, behind fading fences.
Open Water — let go when they've earned it. That's it. That's the animal. That's the harness.
